📖 Parts of Speech the 8 building blocks of English

Every single word in the English language belongs to one of 8 categories called Parts of Speech. Learning them is the foundation of grammar, writing, and communication.

📘 What is a Part of Speech?

A Part of Speech is a category into which a word is placed based on its function in a sentence — not just its meaning. The same word can belong to different categories depending on how it is used.

🔹 Example 1

"I run every morning."
→ run is a Verb (action)

🔹 Example 2

"I went for a run."
→ run is a Noun (thing)

🔹 Example 3

"The run route is long."
→ run is an Adjective (describes route)

Teacher's Note: Never teach parts of speech as "word meanings". Teach them as jobs that words do in a sentence. A word can have multiple jobs!

💡 The 8 Parts of Speech

1. Noun 🔵

Names a person, place, thing, or idea.
Ahmed, Lahore, book, love

2. Pronoun 🟠

Replaces a noun to avoid repetition.
he, she, it, they, we, I

3. Verb 🔴

Shows action or state of being.
run, is, think, jump, seem

4. Adjective 🟡

Describes a noun or pronoun.
big, red, happy, three

5. Adverb 🟢

Describes a verb, adjective, or adverb.
quickly, very, well, here

6. Preposition 🟣

Shows relationship of a noun to another word.
in, on, at, under, between

7. Conjunction 🩵

Joins words, phrases, or clauses.
and, but, or, because, so

8. Interjection 🩷

Expresses sudden emotion or feeling.
wow, oh, alas, hurray, oops

🎯 Why Parts of Speech Matter

  • Better Writing: Choosing the right word type improves clarity.
  • Grammar Rules: Punctuation, tense, and sentence structure all depend on it.
  • Vocabulary: Learn one root word → use it as noun, verb, adjective, adverb.
  • Exam Success: Every English test checks parts of speech.
  • Communication: Understand what others say more precisely.
  • Translation: Urdu/English translation becomes much more accurate.

beauty (Noun)

Her beauty is famous.

beautify (Verb)

They beautify the city.

beautiful (Adjective)

A beautiful flower.

beautifully (Adverb)

She sings beautifully.

🔵 NOUN — Names a person, place, thing, or idea

Definition: A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, animal, or abstract idea. Nouns are the most common type of word in English.

  • Common Noun: General name — boy, city, book
  • Proper Noun: Specific name, always capitalized — Ahmed, Lahore, Quran
  • Concrete Noun: Physical, can be touched — table, apple, dog
  • Abstract Noun: Idea or feeling — love, courage, freedom
  • Countable Noun: Can be counted — two books, three chairs
  • Uncountable Noun: Cannot be counted — water, rice, milk
  • Collective Noun: Group of things — team, family, flock
  • Material Noun: Substance — gold, wood, iron

Proper

Pakistan is beautiful.

Common

The boy is playing.

Abstract

Honesty is the best policy.

Collective

A flock of birds flew.

Common Mistake: Students often capitalize every noun. Only proper nouns take a capital letter.

🟠 PRONOUN — Replaces a noun

Definition: A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition and make sentences smoother.

  • Personal: I, you, he, she, it, we, they
  • Possessive: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs
  • Reflexive: myself, yourself, himself, herself
  • Demonstrative: this, that, these, those
  • Interrogative: who, whom, whose, which, what
  • Relative: who, whom, whose, which, that
  • Indefinite: someone, anybody, everything, none
  • Reciprocal: each other, one another

Personal

She is my sister.

Possessive

That book is mine.

Reflexive

He hurt himself.

Demonstrative

This is my pen.

Common Mistake: Confusing "its" (possessive) with "it's" (it is). Also "their / there / they're".

🔴 VERB — Action or state of being

Definition: A verb is a word that shows an action, occurrence, or state of being. Every complete sentence must have a verb.

  • Action Verb: run, jump, write, eat
  • State Verb: is, am, are, was, seem, appear
  • Transitive Verb: Takes an object — "She reads a book."
  • Intransitive Verb: No object — "He sleeps."
  • Linking Verb: Connects subject to description — "She is happy."
  • Auxiliary (Helping) Verb: be, have, do, will, can, must
  • Modal Verb: can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must
  • Phrasal Verb: Verb + preposition — "give up", "look after"

Action

She writes a letter.

Linking

He is a doctor.

Modal

You must study.

Phrasal

Please look after the baby.

Common Mistake: Missing the verb entirely. Every sentence needs one!

🟡 ADJECTIVE — Describes a noun

Definition: An adjective is a word that describes, modifies, or gives more information about a noun or pronoun.

  • Descriptive: big, red, beautiful, tall
  • Quantitative: some, many, few, several
  • Numeral: one, two, first, second
  • Demonstrative: this, that, these, those
  • Possessive: my, your, his, her, our, their
  • Interrogative: which, what, whose
  • Distributive: each, every, either, neither
  • Proper: Pakistani, English, French

Descriptive

A beautiful flower.

Possessive

My house is big.

Quantitative

Many students came.

Numeral

Three cats are sleeping.

Common Mistake: Adjective order — opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose: "a lovely big old round brown Italian wooden table".

🟢 ADVERB — Describes verb, adjective, or adverb

Definition: An adverb is a word that modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb — telling us how, when, where, why, or to what degree.

  • Manner (how): quickly, slowly, well, badly
  • Time (when): now, yesterday, soon, later
  • Place (where): here, there, everywhere, outside
  • Frequency (how often): always, often, never, sometimes
  • Degree (how much): very, quite, extremely, too
  • Reason (why): therefore, thus, hence
  • Interrogative: when, where, why, how
  • Relative: when, where, why

Manner

He runs quickly.

Time

I will come tomorrow.

Frequency

She always smiles.

Degree

It is very hot.

Common Mistake: Using adjective instead of adverb — "She sings beautiful" ✗ → "She sings beautifully" ✓.

🟣 PREPOSITION — Shows relationship

Definition: A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and another word in the sentence — often indicating place, time, or direction.

  • Place: in, on, at, under, over, behind, between, near
  • Time: at, on, in, before, after, during, since
  • Direction: to, from, into, onto, towards, through
  • Agent: by, with
  • Instrument: with, by
  • Possession: of, with
  • Phrase: in front of, next to, because of, on behalf of

Place

The cat is on the table.

Time

We met at 5 pm.

Direction

She walked into the room.

Phrase

He sat next to me.

Common Mistake: Urdu speakers often say "discuss about" ✗ — correct is "discuss" ✓ (no preposition).

🩵 CONJUNCTION — Joins words or clauses

Definition: A conjunction is a word that connects words, phrases, or clauses together.

  • Coordinating: FANBOYS — for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so
  • Subordinating: because, although, since, unless, when, while, if
  • Correlative: either…or, neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also
  • Conjunctive Adverb: however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless

Coordinating

I like tea and coffee.

Subordinating

She cried because she failed.

Correlative

Either you or I must go.

Conjunctive

He was tired; however, he continued.

Memory Trick: FANBOYS = For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.

🩷 INTERJECTION — Shows sudden emotion

Definition: An interjection is a word or short phrase that expresses sudden emotion or feeling. It is usually followed by an exclamation mark (!).

  • Joy: Hurray! Yay! Wow!
  • Sorrow: Alas! Oh no!
  • Surprise: Oh! What! Really!
  • Pain: Ouch! Ah!
  • Greeting: Hello! Hi! Hey!
  • Annoyance: Ugh! Phew!
  • Attention: Hey! Look! Listen!
  • Mild: well, oh, ah (usually followed by comma)

Joy

Hurray! We won!

Sorrow

Alas! He failed.

Surprise

Wow! What a view!

Pain

Ouch! That hurt.

Common Mistake: Using too many interjections in formal writing. Keep them for creative or conversational writing only.
