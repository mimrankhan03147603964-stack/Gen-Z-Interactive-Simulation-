Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction & Lenz's Law Physics & Electromagnetism

A changing magnetic flux through a conductor loop induces an electromotive force (EMF / voltage). Lenz's Law establishes that the induced current flows in a direction such that its magnetic field opposes the change in flux that created it: Ε = -N · (dΦ / dt).

Fundamental Concepts

  • Magnetic Flux (Φ = B · A · cos θ): The measure of total magnetic field lines passing perpendicular through the cross-sectional area of a loop.
  • Electromotive Force (Ε): The potential difference generated across the terminals of the coil when flux changes over time.
  • Lenz's Law Negative Sign: Conservation of energy ensures that the induced magnetic field resists the motion of the approaching or receding magnet.

Mathematical Derivation & Variables

Ε = -N × (ΔΦ / Δt) = -N × A × (ΔB / Δt)

Where N is the number of coil turns, Φ is magnetic flux (Weber, Wb), t is time in seconds, and Ε is induced voltage (Volts). Faster motion generates higher peak voltage!

Magnetic Field Vectors & Compass Array Mapping Field Visualization

Magnetic fields B form continuous closed loops originating from magnetic North and terminating at magnetic South. Test probes (compasses) always align parallel with the local magnetic vector field.

Magnetic Field Topology

  • North to South Convention: Field lines exit the North pole and enter the South pole, continuing through the magnet interior to form closed loops.
  • Field Line Density: The proximity of lines represents magnetic flux density (B in Tesla). Density is greatest near the pole tips.
  • Non-Intersecting: Magnetic field lines never intersect at any point in space, because the field vector is unique at every coordinate.

Magnetic Dipole Moment

A bar magnet acts as a magnetic dipole with moment m. At distance r along the axial line, the field strength decays with distance cubed: B ∝ 1 / r³.

AC Transformer & Mutual Induction Lab Voltage Transformation

A transformer transfers electrical energy between circuits via mutual magnetic induction. Alternating current in the primary coil generates an oscillating magnetic flux in the ferromagnetic core, inducing an AC voltage in the secondary coil: Vs / Vp = Ns / Np = Ip / Is.

Principles of Mutual Induction

  • Step-Up Transformer (Ns > Np): Secondary turns exceed primary turns, stepping up voltage while reducing secondary current to minimize $I^2R$ grid transmission heat losses.
  • Step-Down Transformer (Ns < Np): Reduces high transmission line voltage down to safe household levels (120V / 240V).
  • Ferromagnetic Laminated Core: Silicon steel laminations concentrate magnetic flux lines while suppressing parasitic eddy current heating.

Ideal Transformer Equations

Vs = Vp × (Ns / Np)

Conservation of energy (assuming 100% ideal efficiency): Pin = Pout ⇒ Vp · Ip = Vs · Is

Lorentz Force & Charged Particle Motion in Magnetic Field Electrodynamics

A charged particle moving with velocity v through a perpendicular magnetic field B experiences a magnetic deflecting force perpendicular to both its velocity and the field vector: F = q · (v × B) (Fleming's Left-Hand Rule).

Perpendicular Work & Circular Orbits

  • Zero Work Done: Because the Lorentz magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity (F ⊥ v), the magnetic field does zero work on the particle; its kinetic energy and speed remain constant while its direction bends!
  • Right-Hand vs Left-Hand Rule: Positive particles (protons) curve in one direction, while negative particles (electrons) curve oppositely.
  • Helical Motion: If velocity has a parallel component along the B-field axis, the particle traces a 3D corkscrew helix along field lines.

Cyclotron Formula

Equating magnetic force to centripetal force: q · v · B = m · v² / r ⇒ r = m · v / (q · B)

Heavy particles (alpha particles) have a much wider turning radius than lightweight electrons!
