📖 Definition

Ohm's Law states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it and inversely proportional to its resistance, provided the temperature remains constant.

V = I × RVoltage (V) = Current (I) × Resistance (R)
Teacher's Note: Formulated by Georg Simon Ohm (1827). It is the fundamental relationship in electrical circuits.

💡 Explanation

Three quantities work together in every circuit:

  • Voltage (V) — measured in Volts (V): The electrical potential push that moves electrons.
  • Current (I) — measured in Amperes (A): The flow rate of electric charge.
  • Resistance (R) — measured in Ohms (Ω): The opposition to electron flow.

📝 Worked Examples

Example 1 — Find V

I = 2 A, R = 5 Ω
V = I × R = 10 V

Example 2 — Find I

V = 12 V, R = 4 Ω
I = V ÷ R = 3 A

Example 3 — Find R

V = 9 V, I = 3 A
R = V ÷ I = 3 Ω
