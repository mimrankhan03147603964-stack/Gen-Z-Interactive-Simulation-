Kinematics — Motion & Graphs

Distance-Time Graphs • Speed-Time Graphs • Free Fall Motion

Definition — Easy English

Kinematics is the branch of physics that describes how objects move — without worrying about why they move. It studies distance, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration.

Important Words

Distance (m) Total path covered by a moving object. It is a scalar — magnitude only.
Displacement (m) Shortest straight-line distance from start to end with direction. Vector.
Speed (m/s) Speed = Distance ÷ Time. Scalar quantity.
Velocity (m/s) Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time. Vector quantity with direction.
Acceleration (m/s²) Rate of change of velocity: a = (v − u) ÷ t.
Free Fall Motion under gravity alone (no air resistance). Acceleration = g = 9.8 m/s² downward.

1 Distance-Time Graph

A Distance-Time graph shows how distance changes with time. Slope equals speed.

Speed = Slope = ΔDistance ÷ ΔTime

2 Speed-Time Graph

A Speed-Time graph shows speed vs time. Slope = acceleration, Area = distance travelled.

Acceleration = Slope   •   Distance = Area under graph

3 Free Fall Motion

Objects falling under gravity alone accelerate downward at g = 9.8 m/s².

v = u + gt   •   h = ut + ½gt²   •   v² = u² + 2gh

Explanation — With Real Life Examples

1. Distance-Time Graph

Straight sloping line = constant speed. Horizontal line = stationary object. Steeper slope = higher speed.

2. Speed-Time Graph

Upward slope = acceleration. Horizontal line = constant speed. Downward slope = deceleration. Area = distance.

3. Free Fall

In vacuum, all objects fall at the exact same rate regardless of mass (hammer and feather drop together).
