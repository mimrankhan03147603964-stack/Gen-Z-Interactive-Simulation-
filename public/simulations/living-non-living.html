Living and Non-Living Things

Discover the characteristics of life, explore offline visual examples, and test your classification skills in an interactive drag-and-drop sandbox.

DEFINITION

Living Things

Living things are organisms that carry out all life processes. They grow, respire, respond to stimuli, reproduce, and need energy and water to survive.

Simple idea: A living thing is alive — it has life.

Examples: Plants, animals, humans, fungi, bacteria.

DEFINITION

Non-Living Things

Non-living things do not perform life processes. They do not grow by themselves, breathe, reproduce, or need food and water to stay alive.

Simple idea: A non-living thing is not alive.

Examples: Rocks, water, toys, cars, buildings.

Key Features of Living Things

Living things share common characteristics that distinguish them from non-living matter.

1. GrowthLiving things grow and develop over time. A seed becomes a tree.
2. MovementAnimals move freely. Plants show directional growth towards sunlight.
3. RespirationLiving organisms release energy from nutrients through respiration.
4. NutritionPlants synthesize food; animals consume food for growth and energy.
5. ResponseLiving things sense and respond to light, temperature, touch, and sound.
6. ReproductionLiving organisms reproduce to produce offspring of their own species.
7. Need WaterWater is vital for cell metabolism, fluid transport, and chemical balance.
8. Need GasesOrganisms exchange atmospheric gases (oxygen and carbon dioxide).
9. Life CycleLiving things undergo stages of birth, maturation, reproduction, and death.
Remember: An engine moves, but movement alone does not make it a living thing.
