Newton's 3 Laws of Motion

Learn how objects move, stop, and push back — with real life examples

Definition — Easy English

In 1687, Sir Isaac Newton formulated three fundamental laws that describe how objects move and interact.

1 Newton's First Law — Law of Inertia

An object stays at rest, or continues to move at a constant speed in a straight line, unless an external force acts on it.

No Net Force → Constant Velocity

2 Newton's Second Law — F = m × a

The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force applied, and inversely proportional to its mass.

F = m × a → a = F ÷ m

3 Newton's Third Law — Action and Reaction

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Forces always occur in matched pairs.

Action Force = − Reaction Force

Explanation — Principles & Real Life

1. Inertia in Action

When a car suddenly stops, your body continues forward due to inertia — which is why seatbelts save lives.

2. Mass & Force Scaling

Pushing an empty supermarket trolley requires far less force than a loaded one to reach the same acceleration.

3. Rockets & Swimming

Rockets expel gas downward; the gas exerts an equal and opposite reaction pushing the rocket upward into space.
