Light Dispersion and Rainbow Formation inside a Water Droplet

Dispersion of Light

White sunlight is a mixture of many colors. Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its colors when it passes through a transparent material such as water or glass.

Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet

Each color bends by a slightly different amount, so the colors fan out.

Rainbow Formation

A rainbow is a circular arc of colors. It forms when sunlight enters millions of raindrops, reflects once inside each drop, and leaves split into colors.

Refraction + Reflection + Refraction

You see a rainbow when the Sun is behind you and rain is in front of you.

What happens inside one droplet?

  • Step 1: White sunlight arrives at the droplet.
  • Step 2: Light slows down in water and bends (refraction). Violet bends most and red bends least, so the colors split.
  • Step 3: The light bounces off the back wall like a mirror (internal reflection).
  • Step 4: It leaves the droplet, bends again, and the colors spread further apart.

Red comes out at about 42° and violet at about 40° from the point opposite the Sun.

Why is the rainbow an arc?

Every drop that sits at the right angle sends one color to your eye. Red drops lie on the outer circle and violet drops on the inner circle.

  • Higher Sun means a lower rainbow. Above 42° no rainbow is visible.
  • Two reflections inside a drop make a fainter double rainbow with colors reversed.
  • You can see the same effect in a prism, a CD, or the mist from a garden hose.
