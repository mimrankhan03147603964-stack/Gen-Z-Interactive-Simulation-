Breathing Process How we take in O₂ and give out CO₂

Watch air travel through the nose, trachea, bronchi and lungs. See oxygen enter the blood and carbon dioxide leave it inside the tiny air sacs called alveoli.

Definition

Breathing is the process of taking air into the lungs (inhalation) and pushing air out of the lungs (exhalation). It brings oxygen (O₂) into the body and removes carbon dioxide (CO₂).

Explanation

  • Inhalation: The diaphragm contracts and moves down. The lungs expand. Air rushes in through the nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea and bronchi.
  • Air reaches the alveoli (air sacs). Oxygen passes into the blood through the thin walls of the capillaries.
  • Exhalation: The diaphragm relaxes and moves up. The lungs get smaller. Air is pushed out.
  • Carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the alveoli and leaves the body with the air.

Key Facts

  • Inhalation brings O₂ IN. Exhalation sends CO₂ OUT.
  • The diaphragm is the main breathing muscle under the lungs.
  • Alveoli are tiny air sacs. Gas exchange happens here.
  • Red blood cells carry oxygen to all parts of the body.
  • The trachea (windpipe) splits into two bronchi, one for each lung.
