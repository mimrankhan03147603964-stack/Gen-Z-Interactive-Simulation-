Rotation (24 Hours): Earth spins eastward on its axis once every 23 hours 56 minutes, creating diurnal day and night cycles.

Revolution (365.25 Days): Earth orbits the Sun in a slightly elliptical orbit at an average orbital speed of 29.78 km/s (107,200 km/h).

Axial Tilt (23.44°): Earth's rotational axis points constantly towards Polaris (North Star) throughout the entire orbit.

Why do we have seasons? Seasons are not caused by distance from the Sun! They are caused entirely by Earth's axial tilt.

When the Northern Hemisphere leans towards the Sun in June, sunlight strikes at a direct 90° angle, producing longer days and Summer.

Six months later in December, the Northern Hemisphere leans away, spreading solar rays over a wider surface area with fewer daylight hours.

• Perihelion: Nearest to Sun (147.1 M km) occurs in early January during Northern Winter!

• Aphelion: Furthest from Sun (152.1 M km) occurs in early July.

• Midnight Sun: Regions above 66.5° N experience 24 hours of daylight around the June Solstice.
