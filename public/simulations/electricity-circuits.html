⚡ Electricity circuits, bulbs, switches & batteries

Build a real circuit with a battery, a switch, wires, and a bulb. Choose series or parallel. Watch electrons flow and the bulb glow.

📖 Definition

Electricity is the flow of electric charge (electrons) through a conductor. A circuit is a closed loop that lets electrons flow from the battery, through the wires, and back.

  • Battery: Pushes electrons (voltage).
  • Wire: Path for electrons (copper).
  • Switch: Opens or closes the circuit.
  • Bulb: Glows when electrons pass through it.
Teacher's Note: Just like water flows in a pipe only when the tap is open, electrons flow only when the circuit is closed (switch ON).

💡 Explanation

Electrons need a complete path. If the switch is open, the path is broken and no current flows — the bulb stays off. Close the switch → electrons flow → bulb glows.

  • Voltage (V): The push from the battery. Unit = volt.
  • Current (I): The flow of electrons. Unit = ampere (A).
  • Resistance (R): Opposition to flow. Unit = ohm (Ω).
  • Ohm's Law: V = I × R.

🧩 Types of Circuits

Series Circuit

One single path. If one bulb breaks, all go off. Same current through all.

Parallel Circuit

Multiple paths. If one bulb breaks, others still glow. Same voltage across all.

Open Circuit

Switch OFF — path is broken. No current flows.

Closed Circuit

Switch ON — complete loop. Current flows freely.

Short Circuit

Wire bypasses the bulb — dangerous, no glow.

Battery

Provides voltage. More cells = more push.
