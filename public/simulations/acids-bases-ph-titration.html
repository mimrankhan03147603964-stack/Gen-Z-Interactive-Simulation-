🧪 The 0–14 pH Scale & Hydrogen Ion Concentration Acid-Base Chemistry Lab

Investigate logarithmic acidity and alkalinity. The pH scale measures the decimal logarithm of hydrogen ion activity: pH = -log₁₀[H⁺]. Acidic solutions (pH < 7) have high [H⁺] ions; basic solutions (pH > 7) have high [OH⁻] hydroxide ions.

Logarithmic Nature of pH

Because pH is logarithmic, each whole pH unit represents a 10-fold change in hydrogen ion concentration. A solution of pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4 and 100 times more acidic than pH 5.

[H⁺] Hydronium Ions

Acids release protons (H⁺) into water, forming hydronium ions (H₃O⁺). Universal indicator glows red/orange.

Neutral Water (pH 7.0)

At 25°C, autoionization gives [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ M, yielding a perfectly balanced neutral pH of 7.

[OH⁻] Hydroxide Ions

Bases accept protons or release OH⁻ ions. Universal indicator shifts to deep royal blue and violet.

Substance pH Spectrum

  • Strong Acids (pH 0–2): Battery Acid (0.8), Stomach Gastric Juice (1.5), Lemon Juice (2.2).
  • Weak Acids & Everyday Items (pH 3–6): Vinegar (2.8), Tomato Juice (4.2), Black Coffee (5.0), Pure Milk (6.6).
  • Weak Bases & Physiological Fluids (pH 7–10): Human Blood (7.4), Sea Water (8.1), Baking Soda (8.5).
  • Strong Bases (pH 11–14): Ammonia Cleaner (11.5), Bleach (12.5), Drain Cleaner NaOH (14.0).

Electrode Sensor Calibration

Remind learners that standard laboratory glass pH electrodes require calibration using two buffer solutions (pH 4.0 and pH 7.0 or pH 10.0) before measurement.

Teacher Tip: Adding water to dilute an acid shifts its pH closer to neutral 7.0, but dilution alone can never turn an acid into a base!

⚗️ Acid-Base Buret Titration & Neutralization Analytical Chemistry Laboratory

Perform a precise quantitative titration. Slowly dispense standardized Sodium Hydroxide (0.10 M NaOH) from a 50 mL graduated buret into 25 mL of 0.10 M Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) containing phenolphthalein indicator. Watch the live pH titration curve plot in real-time!

Teacher Tip: Phenolphthalein transitions from colorless to light pink at the end point (pH ~8.2–10.0), signaling that stoichiometric neutralization is complete.
