⚖️ Balance Model the golden rule of equations

An equation is like a balanced scale. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other. Watch the scale tilt and balance as you perform operations!

📖 Definition

An equation is a mathematical statement that two expressions are equal. The balance model shows an equation as a scale: the left side must always equal the right side.

Teacher's Note: Think of a see-saw. If you add weight to one side, you must add the same to the other to keep it balanced. That is exactly what we do when solving equations!

💡 Golden Rules of Equations

  • Add the same number to both sides
  • Subtract the same number from both sides
  • Multiply both sides by the same number
  • Divide both sides by the same number
  • The equation stays balanced — the solution never changes
  • Goal: get the variable x alone on one side

🧩 Simple Examples

x + 3 = 7

Subtract 3 → x = 4

x − 5 = 2

Add 5 → x = 7

3x = 12

Divide by 3 → x = 4

x / 4 = 5

Multiply by 4 → x = 20

2x + 1 = 9

Subtract 1, ÷2 → x = 4

5x − 3 = 22

Add 3, ÷5 → x = 5

➗ Linear Equations solve for x step by step

Watch how an equation is solved step-by-step. Each operation is applied to both sides to isolate x.

📖 Definition

A linear equation is an equation of the form ax + b = c, where x is the unknown and a, b, c are numbers. It has exactly one solution.

Teacher's Note: Linear means "straight line" — when graphed, these equations make a straight line. The highest power of x is 1.

💡 Method to Solve

  • Step 1: Simplify both sides if needed
  • Step 2: Add or subtract to move constants
  • Step 3: Multiply or divide to isolate x
  • Step 4: Check your answer by substitution

🔢 Worked Examples

🔢 Two-Step Equations two operations to isolate x

Two-step equations require two inverse operations to solve. Learn the order: undo addition/subtraction first, then multiplication/division.

📖 Definition

A two-step equation needs exactly two operations to solve. Example: 3x + 4 = 19. You must subtract 4, then divide by 3.

Teacher's Note: Always undo addition/subtraction FIRST (the "outer" operation), then multiplication/division (the "inner" operation).

💡 Order of Operations (Reverse)

  • Step 1: Undo + or − (move constant to other side)
  • Step 2: Undo × or ÷ (isolate x)
  • Step 3: Check by substituting back
  • Think SADMEG: Subtract, Add, Divide, Multiply, Exponents, Group

🔢 Worked Examples

2x + 5 = 13

−5 → 2x = 8 → x = 4

3x − 7 = 14

+7 → 3x = 21 → x = 7

5x + 2 = 27

−2 → 5x = 25 → x = 5

4x − 6 = 18

+6 → 4x = 24 → x = 6

x/2 + 3 = 8

−3 → x/2 = 5 → x = 10

x/4 − 1 = 4

+1 → x/4 = 5 → x = 20

🔲 Equations with Brackets expand then solve

When equations contain brackets, expand first using the distributive law, then solve.

📖 Definition

An equation with brackets looks like a(x + b) = c. First expand the bracket, then solve.

Teacher's Note: Brackets are like a shopping bag — everything inside must be multiplied by the number outside.

💡 Distributive Law

  • a(b + c) = ab + ac
  • a(b − c) = ab − ac
  • Multiply EVERY term inside by the outside number
  • Watch out for negative signs!
  • After expanding, solve like a normal linear equation

🔢 Worked Examples

2(x + 3) = 14

2x + 6 = 14 → x = 4

3(x − 2) = 9

3x − 6 = 9 → x = 5

4(x + 1) = 20

4x + 4 = 20 → x = 4

5(x − 3) = 10

5x − 15 = 10 → x = 5

2(3x + 1) = 20

6x + 2 = 20 → x = 3

3(2x − 4) = 18

6x − 12 = 18 → x = 5

❓ Unknown on Both Sides collect x terms together

When x appears on both sides, move all x terms to one side and all constants to the other.

📖 Definition

Some equations have the unknown on both sides: ax + b = cx + d. We must gather all x terms on one side.

Teacher's Note: Think of it as moving all the "x boxes" to one side of the balance and all the "number boxes" to the other.

💡 Strategy

  • Step 1: Subtract cx from both sides (move x terms left)
  • Step 2: Subtract b from both sides (move constants right)
  • Step 3: Divide by (a − c) to isolate x
  • Step 4: Check your solution
  • Tip: Always move the SMALLER x coefficient to avoid negatives

🔢 Worked Examples

5x + 2 = 3x + 10

2x = 8 → x = 4

4x − 3 = 2x + 7

2x = 10 → x = 5

7x + 1 = 4x + 13

3x = 12 → x = 4

6x − 5 = 3x + 7

3x = 12 → x = 4

8x + 3 = 5x + 18

3x = 15 → x = 5

9x − 4 = 4x + 16

5x = 20 → x = 4
