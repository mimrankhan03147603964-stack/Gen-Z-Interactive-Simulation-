🌌 Solar System & Planetary Motion

📘 Definition

Planetary Motion is the movement of planets orbiting around a central star (the Sun) in fixed paths called Orbits. The force that keeps planets in their orbits is Gravity.

🌞 The SunThe Sun holds 99.86% of the mass of the Solar System, which is why all planets orbit around it.
🛰️ OrbitEach planet follows a fixed elliptical path around the Sun.
🧲 GravityThe Sun's strong gravity pulls planets inward, while their speed keeps them from falling in.
⏱️ Orbital PeriodThe farther a planet is from the Sun, the slower it moves and the longer its year is.

🔭 Explanation — Kepler's Laws & Physics

Our Solar System follows clear physical laws. Each point is explained separately below:

1️⃣ First Law — Elliptical Orbits

Planets do not move in perfect circles. They move in elliptical (oval-shaped) paths, and the Sun sits at one focus of that ellipse. This means the distance between a planet and the Sun changes throughout the year.

2️⃣ Second Law — Equal Areas

A planet moves faster when it is closer to the Sun (perihelion) and slower when it is farther away (aphelion). Mercury has the most elliptical orbit, so its speed change is very noticeable.

3️⃣ Third Law — Orbital Period and Distance

The cube of the distance (a³) and the square of the orbital period (T²) always have the same ratio. This is why distant planets have very long years — Neptune takes 165 Earth years for one orbit!

4️⃣ Gravity — The Force That Holds Everything

According to Newton's law of gravitation, two objects attract each other with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The Sun is very massive, so its gravity keeps all planets in their orbits.

5️⃣ Effect of Distance from the Sun

Inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are rocky and warm. Outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) are gas and ice giants and are very cold.

6️⃣ Day and Night

Each planet also spins on its own axis. The side facing the Sun has day, and the opposite side has night. This spinning motion creates day/night cycles and seasons.
