🧬 DNA Molecular Structure & Base Pairing Molecular Genetics Lab

Explore the 3D helical structure of Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA). Observe Watson-Crick complementary nitrogenous base pairs: Adenine (A) binds with Thymine (T) via 2 hydrogen bonds, and Guanine (G) binds with Cytosine (C) via 3 hydrogen bonds.

The Double Helix Architecture

DNA stores the genetic code for all living organisms. Two antiparallel strands composed of alternating sugar (deoxyribose) and phosphate groups wind around a central axis, joined by rungs of nitrogenous bases.

Purines & Pyrimidines

Adenine & Guanine (double-ring purines) always pair with Thymine & Cytosine (single-ring pyrimidines).

Hydrogen Bonding

A = T pairs share 2 hydrogen bonds. G ≡ C pairs share 3 hydrogen bonds, making GC-rich DNA thermally stable.

Antiparallel Strands

One strand runs 5' to 3' while the complementary template runs opposite from 3' to 5'.

DNA Replication Mechanism

  • Helicase unzips hydrogen bonds between base pairs, separating the two template strands.
  • DNA Polymerase synthesizes complementary daughter strands following Chargaff's rules (A=T, G=C).
  • Semi-conservative replication ensures each new cell receives one original and one newly synthesized strand.
