🌑 Solar & Lunar Eclipse

Learn how the Sun, Earth, and Moon line up to make an eclipse

📘 Definition

An Eclipse happens when one space object moves into the shadow of another space object. In our Solar System, eclipses happen when the Sun, the Earth, and the Moon line up in a straight line.

🌞 Solar EclipseThe Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth. The Moon blocks the Sun's light. This happens during a New Moon.
🌕 Lunar EclipseThe Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon. The Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. This happens during a Full Moon.
🌑 UmbraThe dark inner part of a shadow. Light is fully blocked here.
🌘 PenumbraThe light outer part of a shadow. Light is only partly blocked here.

🔭 Explanation — How Eclipses Work

1️⃣ The Sun, Earth and Moon Line Up

An eclipse only happens when the Sun, Earth, and Moon come in a straight line. The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5°, so this perfect line-up does not happen every month.

2️⃣ Solar Eclipse — Moon Blocks the Sun

A Solar Eclipse happens when the Moon moves between the Sun and the Earth. The Moon covers the Sun's bright face. This only happens during a New Moon.

3️⃣ Lunar Eclipse — Earth Blocks the Moon

A Lunar Eclipse happens when the Earth moves between the Sun and the Moon. The Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. This only happens during a Full Moon.

4️⃣ Two Parts of a Shadow

  • Umbra — dark center. Light fully blocked.
  • Penumbra — light outer part. Light partly blocked.

5️⃣ Types of Solar Eclipse

  • Total — Moon fully covers the Sun.
  • Partial — Moon covers only part of the Sun.
  • Annular — A bright ring of Sun light stays visible around the Moon.

6️⃣ Types of Lunar Eclipse

  • Total — Moon fully enters Earth's umbra. It looks red (Blood Moon).
  • Partial — Only part of Moon enters umbra.
  • Penumbral — Moon only passes through penumbra. Looks slightly dim.

7️⃣ Why the Moon Looks Red

Sun light passes through Earth's atmosphere and bends. Blue light scatters away, but red light bends toward the Moon. That is why the Moon looks reddish during a total lunar eclipse.

8️⃣ Safety Rule

Never look at the Sun with bare eyes. Always use special eclipse glasses.
