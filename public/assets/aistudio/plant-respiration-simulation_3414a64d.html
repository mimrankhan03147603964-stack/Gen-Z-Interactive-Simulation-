Plant Respiration in Sunlight vs. Complete Darkness

What is respiration in plants?

Respiration is the process that releases energy from food. Every living part of a plant, such as its leaves, stems and roots, uses oxygen to break down sugar (glucose) and release energy. Carbon dioxide and water are the waste products.

Plants respire all the time, in sunlight and in darkness.

Key words

Respiration: using oxygen to get energy from sugar. It happens day and night.
Photosynthesis: making sugar from carbon dioxide and water using light energy. It happens only when there is light.
Stomata: tiny doors on a leaf that let gases in and out.
Mitochondria: the parts of a cell where respiration happens.
Chloroplasts: the green parts of a cell where photosynthesis happens.

In bright sunlight

The plant does both jobs at once. Photosynthesis is much faster than respiration, so the plant makes more oxygen than it uses and takes in more carbon dioxide than it gives out. Overall, oxygen goes out of the leaves and carbon dioxide goes in.

In complete darkness

With no light, photosynthesis stops. Respiration carries on, so the plant only uses oxygen and gives out carbon dioxide. A plant in a sealed jar in the dark slowly uses up the oxygen and fills the jar with carbon dioxide. Limewater turns milky when carbon dioxide is present, which makes a simple test.

The two equations

Respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water + light → glucose + oxygen

At one dim light level the two processes are equal, and no gas leaves or enters the plant. This is called the compensation point.

What to try in the simulation

1. Start in Bright Sunlight and watch the oxygen level rise.
2. Press Complete Darkness and watch oxygen fall while carbon dioxide rises.
3. Press Dim Light to find the balance point.
4. Use Zoom Into Leaf to see the chloroplasts, the mitochondria and the stoma.
5. Check the limewater beaker: it turns milky when there is a lot of carbon dioxide.
