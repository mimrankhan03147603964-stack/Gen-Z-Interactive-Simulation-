Photosynthesis How flowering plants make food

Watch a real flowering plant absorb sunlight, water, and CO₂ — and release oxygen and glucose. Explore the full process through an interactive and animated simulation.

Definition

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food (glucose) using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide. Oxygen is released as a by-product.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Explanation

  • Roots take in water (H₂O) from the soil. Water goes up the stem through the xylem.
  • Leaves take in carbon dioxide (CO₂) from the air through tiny holes called stomata.
  • Chlorophyll inside the chloroplast captures sunlight energy.
  • Light Reactions (in the thylakoids) split water and make energy and oxygen.
  • Calvin Cycle (in the stroma) uses CO₂ and energy to make glucose.
  • Oxygen goes out into the air. Glucose is the food for the plant.

Key Facts

  • Chlorophyll is the green pigment that absorbs sunlight.
  • Chloroplasts are the "kitchens" of the plant cell.
  • More light, water, and CO₂ means faster photosynthesis (up to a limit).
  • Photosynthesis gives us the oxygen we breathe.
  • Cold and cloudy weather slows photosynthesis down.
