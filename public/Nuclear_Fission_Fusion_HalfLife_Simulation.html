Nuclear Fission, Fusion & Half-Life Decay

Nuclear Fission

Fission means splitting. A heavy nucleus, such as Uranium-235, is hit by a neutron and breaks into two smaller nuclei. It also releases 2 or 3 new neutrons and a huge amount of energy.
  • Used in nuclear power stations
  • Can start a chain reaction

Nuclear Fusion

Fusion means joining. Two light nuclei, such as hydrogen isotopes, join to make a heavier nucleus (helium) and release even more energy per kilogram.
  • Powers the Sun and all stars
  • Needs millions of degrees

Half-Life Decay

Some atoms are unstable and decay by giving out radiation. The half-life is the time taken for half of the unstable atoms in a sample to decay.
  • After 1 half-life: 1/2 is left
  • After 2 half-lives: 1/4 is left

How fission works

  1. A slow neutron hits a Uranium-235 nucleus.
  2. The nucleus splits into Barium-141 and Krypton-92.
  3. 2 or 3 neutrons and about 200 MeV of energy are released.
  4. The new neutrons hit more nuclei: a chain reaction.
  5. Control rods absorb neutrons to keep the reaction safe.

How fusion works

  1. Deuterium and Tritium nuclei are both positive, so they repel each other.
  2. Very high temperature makes them move fast enough to get close.
  3. They fuse into Helium-4 and a fast neutron.
  4. About 17.6 MeV of energy is released.

How half-life works

Each atom decays at a random moment, but a big sample decays in a very predictable way.
  • Carbon-14: 5,730 years (dating old wood and bones)
  • Iodine-131: 8 days (thyroid medicine)
  • Cobalt-60: 5.27 years (sterilising medical tools)
  • Uranium-238: 4.5 billion years (dating rocks)
