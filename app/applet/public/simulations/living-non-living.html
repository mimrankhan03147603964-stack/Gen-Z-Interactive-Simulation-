DEFINITION

Living Things

Living things are organisms that carry out all life processes. They grow, respire, respond to stimuli, reproduce, and need energy and water to survive.

Simple idea: A living thing is alive — it has cellular life.

Examples: Plants, animals, humans, fungi, bacteria.

DEFINITION

Non-Living Things

Non-living things do not perform life processes. They do not grow by themselves, breathe, reproduce, or need food and water to stay alive.

Simple idea: A non-living thing is not alive.

Examples: Rocks, water, toys, cars, buildings.

Key Features of Living Things

Living things share common characteristics that distinguish them from non-living matter.

1. GrowthLiving organisms develop over time. A seedling grows into a towering tree.
2. MovementAnimals move freely. Plants orient directional growth toward sunlight.
3. RespirationCells release chemical energy from nutrients through respiration.
4. NutritionAutotrophs make glucose; heterotrophs ingest food for metabolism.
5. ResponseOrganisms sense stimuli like heat, touch, and light phototropism.
6. ReproductionSpecies generate viable offspring to perpetuate genetic lineages.
7. Need WaterCellular cytoplasm, enzymatic catalysts, and nutrient diffusion depend on H₂O.
8. Need GasesAerobic respiration requires oxygen; photosynthesis requires carbon dioxide.
9. Life CycleLiving things undergo gestation, maturation, reproduction, and mortality.
Remember: A vehicle engine moves, but mechanical movement alone does not make it a living organism.
